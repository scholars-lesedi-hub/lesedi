Books in the rain

Naledi proposed keeping the market gates open until noon so shoppers would have time to browse the free book stall. By eight, the wind was pulling at the banner like a sail with nowhere to go. Then rain threaded between the stalls. The volunteers set empty produce crates under the boxes of books to lift them clear of the water.

When the rain eased, a boy picked up an atlas. He traced a river with one finger, put the book back and walked towards the gate. A moment later he returned. “Can I really keep it?” he asked. Naledi nodded. The boy opened the cover again. To him, the map was a promise. By noon, most of the books were still on the stall. Naledi watched him carry the atlas home and decided the morning had been worth it.

Optional writing task and 8-mark guide

In 120–160 words, write a short report for the school newsletter about the book stall. Explain the challenge the volunteers faced, how they responded, and why the event mattered. Use your own words.

Content and organisation (4 marks): award 1 mark each for explaining the weather problem, describing the volunteers’ response, supporting why the stall mattered with a detail, and organising the report logically. Language (4 marks): award 1 mark each for a tone suited to a school newsletter, precise vocabulary in your own words, clear sentence construction, and mostly accurate spelling and punctuation. Mark this separately from the digital reading score.
