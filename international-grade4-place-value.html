Teacher lesson plan

Suggested duration: 60 minutes. This is a Lesedi lesson allocation, not the full official topic allocation.

Lesson outcomes

Learners identify digit values, represent four-digit numbers, compare quantities and explain their decisions. Outcomes here are Lesedi wording, not official objective codes.

Resources and preparation

Prepare digit cards 0–9, counters or bundles, paper and a four-column place-value chart. Draw a chart on the board if printing is unavailable. Check that learners can group ten ones into a ten and ten tens into a hundred.

Place-value example: 5,082

Teaching sequence

  1. Warm-up · 5 min: Count in tens from 120 to 170 and back. Ask what changes and what stays the same.
  2. Model · 12 min: Build 5,082 in the chart. Explain why the zero keeps the hundreds column empty. Compare the digit 8 with its value, 80. Model 5,082 = 5,000 + 80 + 2.
  3. Guided practice · 10 min: Learners build 3,406 and 3,460. Compare from left to right. Ask why 3,460 is greater even though the same digits appear.
  4. Pair activity · 10 min: Give pairs 1, 0, 6 and 3. Make the greatest and smallest four-digit numbers without repeating digits. Discuss why zero cannot lead a four-digit number. Answers: 6,310 and 1,036.
  5. Independent exercise · 18 min: Complete the ten-question practice digitally or on paper. This is informal practice; two marks per correct response and zero for an incorrect response.
  6. Exit check · 5 min: Explain the role of zero in 7,025 and expand it. Expected: zero hundreds; 7,000 + 20 + 5.

Support, extension and feedback

For support, begin with three-digit numbers, use bundles, and leave the labelled chart visible. Read prompts aloud when appropriate without revealing the answer. For extension, ask learners to explain how moving a digit one column left changes its value. When a learner confuses 8 and 80, rebuild the number with tens bundles before retrying.

Suggested scheme-of-work entry

Term 1 · Opening whole-number block · Place value and comparison · 1 suggested lesson · Chart, digit cards and pair work · Evidence: exercise responses and exit explanation. Schedule further lessons for the remaining counting requirements and rounding; this lesson does not complete that block.
